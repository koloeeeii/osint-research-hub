# Case 001 — Operation False Ticket

**Type:** Fictional training simulation  
**Role:** Entry-level criminal intelligence analyst (simulated)  
**Status:** Open / analysis not submitted  
**Suggested duration:** 45–90 minutes

> **Important:** The following incidents, place names, seller aliases, and payment IDs are fabricated for this exercise. They are not real case reports, and no real account should be investigated.

## Intelligence requirement

Three complaints describe suspected online ticket fraud. Assess whether any incidents could be linked to the same operation, rank the significance of the evidence, consider alternative explanations, and identify what information would strengthen or weaken your assessment.

## Source packet: three fictional reports

### Incident A — November 2

- Location: **Northbridge** (fictional)
- Seller alias: **SeatNowDeals**
- Product: Concert tickets
- Listing phrase: **"Released from sponsor hold"**
- Payment identifier: **PAY-417**
- Reported outcome: Buyer paid; tickets were not received.

### Incident B — November 5

- Location: **Lakeside** (fictional)
- Seller alias: **RushSeatMarket**
- Product: Football tickets
- Listing phrase: **"Released from sponsor hold"**
- Payment identifier: **PAY-417**
- Reported outcome: Buyer paid; tickets were not received.

### Incident C — November 8

- Location: **Northbridge** (fictional)
- Seller alias: **DealsSeat24**
- Product: Concert tickets
- Listing phrase: **"Limited time availability"**
- Payment identifier: **PAY-882**
- Reported outcome: Buyer paid; tickets were not received.

## Assignment

1. Which incidents **might** be connected? Distinguish a plausible lead from an established link.
2. Which shared and divergent features are the strongest evidence? Why?
3. What alternative explanations fit the information?
4. What is your confidence level (low, moderate, or high), and what limits it?
5. What **lawfully obtainable** additional evidence would be most useful?

## Deliverables

- A concise, one-page analytical assessment
- A documented evidence log
- A short list of intelligence gaps and next investigative questions

**Current work products:** [Evidence log (CSV)]({{ '/investigations/001-fraud-pattern-analysis/evidence-log.csv' | relative_url }}) · [Assessment worksheet]({{ '/investigations/001-fraud-pattern-analysis/assessment.md' | relative_url }})

The worksheet is intentionally uncompleted. It is better to demonstrate independent reasoning than to publish an invented conclusion.

## Assessment criteria

A strong response distinguishes shared identifiers from merely common scam tactics, avoids attributing the incidents to a real person, makes room for competing hypotheses, calibrates confidence to available evidence, and specifies how each uncertainty could be resolved.
