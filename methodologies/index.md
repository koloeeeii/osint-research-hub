# Research & Intelligence Methodologies

The aim is a repeatable, auditable process—not merely finding interesting facts.

## Six-stage workflow

1. **Define the intelligence question.** Narrow a broad topic to a question that can be answered with evidence.
2. **Scope the collection.** List lawful sources, time period, required variables, and exclusion criteria.
3. **Collect and preserve references.** Record the URL, source, publication date, access date, and relevant excerpt or observation. Respect copyright and privacy.
4. **Verify and corroborate.** Seek independent primary sources; distinguish claims, direct observations, and third-party reporting.
5. **Analyze competing hypotheses.** Compare the evidence for and against alternative interpretations; note missing evidence and possible bias.
6. **Write a calibrated assessment.** Lead with the judgment, explain evidentiary support, state confidence and limitations, and identify next questions.

## Essential distinctions

| Category | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Source fact | What a document or record actually states |
| Verified finding | A claim corroborated to the required standard |
| Inference | A reasoned interpretation beyond the directly observed facts |
| Hypothesis | An explanation to be tested, not presumed true |
| Intelligence gap | Missing information that could alter the assessment |
| Confidence | How strongly the quality, consistency, and completeness of evidence support the judgment |

## Analytical discipline

- **Avoid circular sourcing:** Multiple articles repeating one original report are not independent corroboration.
- **Do not equate correlation with common control:** A shared phrase or behavior alone rarely proves the same actor.
- **Distinguish allegations from findings:** A criminal complaint or indictment contains allegations; a verdict, plea, or judgment has a different evidentiary status.
- **Separate probability from confidence:** How likely an explanation is differs from how strongly the available evidence supports an assessment.
- **Keep an audit trail:** Another reader should be able to retrace each substantive finding.
